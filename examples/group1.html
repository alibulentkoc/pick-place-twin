The design path in 18 steps

Each step has the Group 1 (cantaloupe) numbers. Point at a value with a dotted line to see it in the twin.

  1. Know your fruit. Write down its weight and diameter. Add the gripper weight. Group 1: cantaloupe 4 lb, 6 in diameter. Gripper 25 lb. Load = 4 + 25 = 29 lb.
  2. Know the field. How far apart are the rows, and the plants in a row? The fruit lie in a band centered on the row; that band is your pick width. Steer so that the arm center is over the row center. The space between the bands is for the tractor wheels. Group 1: rows 6 ft (72 in) apart, plants 2 ft apart. Fruit band = 48 in: from 24 in left to 24 in right of the row center (x = -24 to +24 in). That leaves 24 in for the wheels.
  3. Know where the fruit goes. Find how high the tip must be to grab the fruit, and where the bin is. The bin rides on a low cart beside the band. Group 1: to grab, the tip must be the grip length above the fruit center: 3 + 12 = high. The bin is a 24 in bin on a 12 in deck: rim 36 in high, starting right of the row center. To drop, the fruit bottom must clear the rim by 4 in: 36 + 4 + 3 + 12 = high.

    1. Weigh the arm with a full bin: 220 + 64 + 29 + 95 + 20 = 428 lb (the frame weight is an estimate).
    2. Where its weight acts: about 24 in behind the hitch lift points (estimate).
    3. What the hitch can hold: 1,433 lb x 24 in = 34,392 in-lb.
    4. What the arm needs: 428 x 24 = 10,272 in-lb = 30% of the limit: PASS.
    5. Front axle with no arm: 40% of 2,734 lb = 1,093.6 lb.

    7. What is left: 1,093.6 - 297.5 = 796.1 lb = 29% of the weight, more than 20%: PASS. No front weights are necessary.
  17. Test it in FluidSIM. Both circuits move the cylinders and hold the fruit.
  18. Write it up. Every number, with where it came from.

The trials as cards, with the reasons

How we chose the frame

We started from the tractor start values and changed one value at a time. Each card below is one trial. The five design values of the trial are in the gray boxes; the value that we changed in that trial is yellow. Under them are the three checks of the twin, and why the trial passed or failed. Select "Show in the twin" to load the trial into the twin and see the result yourself.

The rule behind every result. A cylinder can be no shorter than its retracted length L_min and no longer than L_min + S. The tip can reach a point only if both cylinder lengths to that point are in this range. Each length is L = √(Δx² + Δy²), from the pivot of the cylinder to the tip. The pivots are at x = -s/2 and x = +s/2, at the bar height H.

3.3 Cylinder forces at the worst point

Why the force is only along the cylinder. Each cylinder has a pin at each end. No load acts between the pins (we ignore the weight of the cylinder). This type of member is a two-force member. It can only push or pull along the line between its pins.

Free-body diagram of the tip. Three forces act on the tip: the load WW down, and the two cylinder forces along the cylinders. TT is positive when the cylinder pulls (tension).

Triangle ratios. Each cylinder is the long side of a right triangle from the tip to its pivot. The horizontal leg is Δx\Delta x = pivot x - tip x. The vertical leg is Δy\Delta y = H - tip y. A force along the cylinder splits like the sides of its triangle: the horizontal part is TΔx/LT\,\Delta x / L, and the vertical part is TΔy/LT\,\Delta y / L. For equilibrium, the sum of the forces in each direction is zero:

T1Δx1L1+T2Δx2L2=0T1Δy1L1+T2Δy2L2=WT_1 \frac{\Delta x_1}{L_1} + T_2 \frac{\Delta x_2}{L_2} = 0 \qquad T_1 \frac{\Delta y_1}{L_1} + T_2 \frac{\Delta y_2}{L_2} = W

Triangle ratios at the drop point (54.5, 55). The pivots are at (-8, 72) and (8, 72):

Δx1L1=−8in−54.5in64.77in=−0.9649Δy1L1=72in−55in64.77in=0.2625\frac{\Delta x_1}{L_1} = \frac{-8\ \text{in} - 54.5\ \text{in}}{64.77\ \text{in}} = -0.9649 \qquad \frac{\Delta y_1}{L_1} = \frac{72\ \text{in} - 55\ \text{in}}{64.77\ \text{in}} = 0.2625

Δx2L2=8in−54.5in49.51in=−0.9392Δy2L2=72in−55in49.51in=0.3434\frac{\Delta x_2}{L_2} = \frac{8\ \text{in} - 54.5\ \text{in}}{49.51\ \text{in}} = -0.9392 \qquad \frac{\Delta y_2}{L_2} = \frac{72\ \text{in} - 55\ \text{in}}{49.51\ \text{in}} = 0.3434

The inches cancel. Thus, the ratios have no units.

3.4 Areas

The cap end has the full piston area: a circle with diameter DD. The rod end has the annulus area: the piston area minus the rod area.

Ac=πD24=π(2in)24=π×4in24=3.142in2A_c = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi (2\ \text{in})^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \times 4\ \text{in}^2}{4} = 3.142\ \text{in}^2

3.5 Pressures

3.6 Speeds

Oil is almost incompressible. Thus, the volume of oil that flows in each second is equal to the volume that the piston moves through in each second (continuity): Q=vAQ = v A, thus v=Q/Av = Q / A. First, convert the flow rate to in3/s. Use 1 gal = 231 in3 and 1 min = 60 s:

Q=6.3galmin×231in3gal×1min60s=6.3×23160in3s=1455.360in3s=24.26in3sQ = 6.3\ \frac{\text{gal}}{\text{min}} \times 231\ \frac{\text{in}^3}{\text{gal}} \times \frac{1\ \text{min}}{60\ \text{s}} = \frac{6.3 \times 231}{60}\ \frac{\text{in}^3}{\text{s}} = \frac{1455.3}{60}\ \frac{\text{in}^3}{\text{s}} = 24.26\ \frac{\text{in}^3}{\text{s}}

How a counterbalance valve opens. The spring of the valve keeps it closed up to its setting psetp_{set}. Two pressures push the valve open: the load pressure on the poppet, and the pilot pressure from the other cylinder line. The pilot pressure acts on an area that is RR times larger (RR is the pilot ratio). The valve opens when:

pload+Rppilot≥pset⇒ppilot=pset−ploadRp_{load} + R\, p_{pilot} \geq p_{set} \quad \Rightarrow \quad p_{pilot} = \frac{p_{set} - p_{load}}{R}

Thus, the load pressure alone cannot open the valve. A small pilot pressure lowers the load at a controlled speed.

Setting. The rule sets the valve 30% above the maximum holding pressure. Thus, a bump in the field cannot open the valve:

1.3×ph2=1.3×172.4psi=224.1psi≈224psi1.3×ph1=1.3×102.2psi=132.9psi≈133psi1.3 \times p_{h2} = 1.3 \times 172.4\ \text{psi} = 224.1\ \text{psi} \approx 224\ \text{psi} \qquad 1.3 \times p_{h1} = 1.3 \times 102.2\ \text{psi} = 132.9\ \text{psi} \approx 133\ \text{psi}

9. Model limits and failure modes (owners: all)

Failure modes. Table W3-2 (week 3) gives five failures, their effect, the protection and the check. The most important protection is the dual counterbalance valves on the cylinder ports.

Model limits. The twin and FluidSIM use static loads. They leave out: the acceleration of the arm and the fruit, field bumps, the weight of the cylinders, the pressure losses in the hoses and valves, and the change of force with position (FluidSIM applies a constant load). Acceleration and bumps can add 50% or more to the forces on mobile equipment. Our pressures are less than 20% of the limit, so the safety factor of 2 and this margin cover them. The cycle time has only 1.5 s of margin (0.42 s with cold oil), so the real cycle can be longer than 8 s.

What if. A 4 in higher bin rim raises the forces by 37%. A 20% lower flow rate leaves only 0.42 s of cycle time margin. A 20% heavier crop raises the forces by only 2.8%.
