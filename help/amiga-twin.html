Amiga watermelon twin

A battery-powered hydraulic arm on a farm-ng Amiga rover. The rover drives along a watermelon row, the camera finds each fruit, and the arm picks it into a side tray or onto a conveyor that fills a deck bin.

The panel, top to bottom

The panel reads in the order you use it. The toolbar stays pinned at the top while you scroll.

Toolbar
E-stop, Pause, Speed (1x to 8x), Reset rig, Reach region on or off, and the status line. The status line always says what the machine is doing.
Rover setup
Remove harvest module lifts the whole harvest unit (frame, arm, cylinders, camera, power unit, conveyor and bin) onto its own stands. The bare Amiga can then drive away for other tasks. Install harvest module drives the rover back and locks the unit on again.
1. Plant watermelons
Choose how many, the leaf cover (None, Over each watermelon, or Whole row), the shape (Round or Elongated) and the leaf coverage from 0 to 100%. Then press Plant. Fruit is placed ahead of the rover, inside the arm's reach.
2. Pick them
Choose where the fruit goes, whether to clear leaves first, the hardware, wrist rotation, and whether the camera must find fruit while driving. Then press Start: drive along the row and pick. The help line under the choices describes exactly what will happen.
3. Manual control
Hold-buttons for each cylinder and for driving, the gripper, home, a point-to-point move (with camera or by timing), a wrist angle slider, and sliders that set the cylinder lengths directly.

Choices in step 2

What each choice changes
ChoiceEffect
In the side trayThe arm swings each watermelon over and sets it in a tray next to the arm.
In the deck binThe conveyor slides down, the arm sets the fruit on the belt, and the belt carries it into the bin on the deck (12 places for round fruit).
Clear leaves before each pickThe blue air knife in front of the arm blows leaves aside while there are leaves in the field. If the first camera look is poor, a puff at the gripper clears the spot before a second look.
Basic: on/off valvesSeries valves move one cylinder at a time, guided by the camera.
UpgradedProportional valves in parallel move both cylinders together along smooth straight-line paths, guided by position sensors on the cylinders.
Wrist rotationA worm gearmotor turns the jaws to close across the narrow side of an elongated watermelon. Adds 1.5 in below the tip.
Find watermelons with the camera while drivingThe rover no longer knows where the fruit is. The camera searches ahead, yellow rings mark candidates, and they turn blue once confirmed. Some fruit can be missed and leaf piles can cause false stops.

Demo scenes

Start each scene from a fresh field: press Remove all watermelons, then Plant. Speed 4x works well.

  1. Basic harvest. Round, no leaves, deck bin. The full loop: drive, find, pick, convey, bin.
  2. Realistic canopy. Whole row at 80%, clearing on, find while driving on. Watch the rings, the air knife and the found, missed and false-stop counts in Results.
  3. Fruit shape. Elongated, wrist off, then on. Without the wrist many fruit are skipped as too wide.
  4. Smoother hardware. Basic, then Upgraded. Compare the motion and the valve openings in the live circuit.
  5. Platform reuse. Remove the harvest module and drive the bare rover.

Readouts and results

Readouts are grouped under Arm, Workspace, Gripper, Hydraulics, Camera, Canopy, Harvest, Power and battery, and Stability. Results keep running totals: picks placed and attempted, cycle time, grab error, gripper hits blocked, picks refused by checks, energy per pick and picks per battery charge.

The live hydraulic circuit

With Basic hardware the circuit shows the series on/off valves. With Upgraded hardware it switches to the parallel circuit: pressure compensators, proportional valves, load-sense shuttles, counterbalance valves, cylinders and string pots, all live. Open Parts, ports and hoses under it for part numbers, cavities, port connections and hose sizes worked out from your settings.
