How it works

The physics and the control behind the twin, in the sequence that the machine uses them.

The equations on this page do not contain unit constants. Before you use an equation, convert all values to one set of units, for example in, lbf and s.

Kinematics: two lengths set the tip

Each cylinder turns on a pivot below the bar. The tip is the point where the two rod ends connect. For two lengths L1 and L2, the tip is where two circles cross. Each circle has its center at a pivot. For a tip position that you want, each cylinder length is the distance from its pivot to that point.

The shaded reach region shows each tip position that the two cylinders can reach in their stroke. Its color shows the cylinder force for each pound of load.

The work path and the worst point

The twin finds the worst point on the work path. The work path is each reachable tip position from the pick line (grasp height, across the pick width) up to the drop height, and across to the drop point. At each position, the twin calculates the two cylinder forces. The worst point is the position where a force is the maximum. The forces increase when the two cylinders are almost parallel. Thus, the worst point is often the drop point above the bin.

Forces and pressures

Each cylinder has a pin at each end. Thus, the force in each cylinder is along its line. The force balance at the tip gives the two forces for a load W. A cylinder that pulls holds the load with the oil in its rod end: p = F / Ar, where Ar is the annulus area. A cylinder that pushes holds the load with the oil in its cap end: p = F / Ac, where Ac is the piston area. The design force is 2 times this force (safety factor 2).

Counterbalance valves

A counterbalance valve stays closed up to its setting pset. It opens when pload + R ppilot is equal to or more than pset. R is the pilot ratio. Thus, the load pressure alone cannot open the valve, and a small pilot pressure lowers the load at a controlled speed. The pilot pressure necessary to lower the load is (pset - pload) / R.

Flow rate and speed

Cylinder speed is v = Q / A. First, convert the flow rate to in3/s: multiply gpm by 231 in3/gal and divide by 60 s/min. During extension, oil fills the cap end (the full piston area). During retraction, oil fills the smaller rod end. Thus, retraction is faster at the same flow rate. The twin adds a flow error to show why timed moves miss the target.

Camera pipeline

  1. Find the pixel position (u, v) of the object in the image.
  2. Measure its depth Z from the stereo disparity.
  3. Change the pixel position and the depth to camera coordinates.
  4. Turn and move the camera coordinates to rig coordinates. Use the tilt, the aim and the mount position of the camera.

The Camera tab in Live equations shows each step with the current numbers, including noise and delay.

Control with on/off valves

The controller compares the tip position from the camera with the target 15 times each second. It opens one valve at a time. The camera delay and noise cause overshoot. Thus, the deadband and the stepped descent are important.
